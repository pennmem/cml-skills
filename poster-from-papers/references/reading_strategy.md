# Reading strategy

The deep-reading approach for a poster is almost identical to the one for a slide deck. The main reference lives in `beamer-from-papers/references/reading_strategy.md` — read that first.

## Deltas specific to posters

A few things differ when the output is a poster, not a deck:

### Time budget

Posters are faster to produce than decks because the output is smaller. A poster from a single paper should take:

- 5–10 minutes on the draft text (same as a talk)
- 2–3 minutes per figure with vision, but only for the 3–5 figures you're keeping (see `figure_selection.md`). You don't need to study every figure in the paper.
- 2–3 minutes on the bib
- No multi-paper synthesis pass (posters are almost always single-paper)

Total: 20–40 minutes of reading before you start laying out.

### What to look for that you wouldn't for a talk

**The abstract is gold.** Abstracts are pre-compressed to ~150–250 words. That's almost exactly the target size for a poster's motivation + methods + key result combined. When drafting poster blocks, start from the abstract's phrasing and cut down from there — often you can lift sentences verbatim.

**The first paragraph of the discussion is gold.** It's the paper telling you what the headline claim is. Your conclusions block should paraphrase it.

**Figure captions tell you how to frame results.** The "key result" sentence of each caption (usually the second or third sentence) is what you want on the poster, not the full caption.

### What to ignore

- All literature review paragraphs in the introduction (the second through Nth paragraphs — only the first paragraph's framing matters)
- Methods subsections that don't produce any of the figures you're keeping
- Discussion paragraphs after the first — those usually situate the work in broader literature, which you don't have room for

### When `memory-publications` is installed

Read `memory-publications/references/domain-knowledge.md` once at the start of the reading pass, same as for beamer-from-papers. It's less critical for a poster (you have less text to get right) but still worth doing — especially for correct terminology in the conclusions block, where a wrong term jumps out.

Also, if the paper is one of the user's active manuscripts, check `memory-publications/references/current-projects.md`. It sometimes has a note about what aspect of the paper the user wants to emphasize for a specific venue (e.g., "for SfN talk, lean methodology-forward"). Obey that if present.
